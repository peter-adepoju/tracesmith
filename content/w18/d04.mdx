Knowing who to send something to is a skill people learn slowly and painfully, by sending things to the wrong place. A matrix shortens that to an afternoon.

## Build the matrix

For your own product, fill in a table like this:

| Problem type | Owner | Channel | How fast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payment declines at scale | Payments / platform | `#inc-payments`, tag on-call | Immediately, P1 |
| One declined payment | Support resolves | — | — |
| Settlement or payout missing | Payments operations | Ticket, tag the duty lead | Same day |
| Webhook delivery failing | Platform | `#inc-platform` | Immediately if more than one partner |
| UI broken, no request sent | Front end | Tracker, link the ticket | Next working day unless blocking |
| 5xx from an endpoint | Back end, with request id | Tracker, P2 | Same day |
| Data wrong in a record | Back end + payments ops | Ticket, both | Same day, never edit it yourself |
| Suspected fraud | Risk / compliance | Their channel only | Immediately, and tell nobody else |
| Suspected security issue | Security | Security channel only | Immediately, never a public ticket |
| Feature request | Product | Backlog | No commitment |
| Documentation wrong | Whoever owns the docs | Small PR or ticket | When you can |

<Callout kind="job">
If your team has no matrix, build one in your first month by asking. Three questions per row, to whoever knows: *who owns this, where do I send it, how fast do you want to hear?*

Then share it. Support agents will use it, new joiners will use it, and it is one of the clearest early contributions you can make — because the cost of mis-routing is paid by everybody.
</Callout>

## Two rows that are different from the others

**Suspected fraud** and **suspected security issues** do not follow the normal path.

Both go to one named team, through their channel, and **nowhere else** — not the general engineering channel, not a public ticket, not a group chat. For fraud there may be a legal requirement not to tip off the account holder; for security, the report is exploit instructions, as week 14 said.

If you are unsure whether something is one of these, treat it as if it is. The cost of over-caution is one extra conversation.

## Every escalation carries impact

Four numbers, always:

- **How many** customers
- **Since when**, and whether it is growing
- **How much money**, where money is involved
- **Whether there is a workaround**

> Three merchants, since 07:50 this morning, approximately NGN 2.4m of attempted volume, no workaround for card — transfer still works.

One line, and the receiving team can prioritise without a conversation.

## While it is escalated

The ticket is still yours. Escalating is not handing over the customer.

**Own the relationship.** The customer talks to you, not to the engineer. Shielding engineering from direct customer contact is part of the job.

**Keep updating** on the schedule you promised, even with nothing new.

**Chase, on a cadence.** For a P1, every 30 minutes is reasonable. For a P3, daily. Not every ten minutes — that slows the fix and spends goodwill you will need.

**Translate in both directions.** The customer's words into technical facts; the engineer's answer into plain language.

<Callout kind="key">
**An escalated ticket with no owner on the support side is how things disappear.**

The engineer thinks support is handling the customer. Support thinks engineering is handling the ticket. Nobody is, and three days later the customer asks what happened.

One named person on each side, always.
</Callout>

## Escalating a priority

Sometimes the ticket is being worked but not fast enough. That is also an escalation, and it has a shape:

> **Raising the priority on INC-204.** It has been four hours and three merchants still cannot take card payments. Estimated NGN 2.4m of attempted volume so far, growing. One merchant has asked about compensation, which makes this a commercial issue as well as a technical one.
>
> I am not asking anyone to work faster — I am asking whether this needs more people on it, or whether we should be telling merchants to expect this to run into tomorrow so they can plan.

That is not a complaint. It names a decision that needs making and gives the information to make it, which is what escalating a priority actually means.
