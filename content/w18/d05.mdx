A ticket closed too early reopens, and a reopened ticket costs far more than the five minutes of verification would have — in time, and in the customer's confidence that you check things.

## Closure criteria, written at the start

Decide what done means before you begin, in the ticket:

> **Closed when:** the refund shows `settled` in our system **and** the customer confirms it has reached their bank.

Both halves. Our system saying `settled` is not the customer having their money, and the gap between those two is exactly where the next ticket comes from.

Good criteria are testable:

| Vague | Testable |
| --- | --- |
| Payment issue resolved | Payment `PAY-8841` shows `successful` and the order exists in the merchant's system |
| Webhook fixed | Three consecutive deliveries return 200 and the partner confirms receipt |
| Customer happy | Customer has confirmed they can complete a payment |
| Bug fixed | Fix deployed, retested on production by me, merchant confirms |

## Verify yourself

Week 11's rule, in support clothing: **only you close it, and only after you have checked.**

Not because the deploy went out. Not because an engineer said it was fixed. Not because the status changed. Because you looked.

<Callout kind="watch">
**"It should be working now" is not verification.** It is a hope with a timestamp.

Check the actual record, the actual response, the actual behaviour. For a payment: look the transaction up. For a webhook: look at the delivery log. For a UI bug: open it yourself.

Thirty seconds, and it is the difference between a closure that holds and one that reopens with an unhappy customer attached.
</Callout>

## Confirm with the customer before closing

Two reasons, and the second is the one people underestimate.

1. They may still be experiencing it. Your record can look correct while their reality has not changed.
2. **Closing without telling them reads as being dropped.** A silently closed ticket is indistinguishable from a forgotten one.

> I have checked and the refund of NGN 48,500 released this morning — reference `REF-2291`. It should reach your account by Thursday.
>
> I will close this now, but it is not a door shutting: reply here any time and it reopens with all the history, so you will not have to explain it again.

That last sentence removes the anxiety that makes customers resist closure.

## The closing message

Four parts:

1. **What was wrong**, in one sentence, in plain language
2. **What was done**
3. **Anything they need to do**, or explicitly nothing
4. **How to come back**

> **What happened:** your payment went through but our system did not manage to tell the shop, so the order was never created.
>
> **What we did:** created the order manually (number 8841, dispatching today) and fixed the delivery problem so it will not recur for your next order.
>
> **Anything you need to do:** nothing. You were charged once and only once; I have checked.
>
> If anything about this order does not look right, reply here and it comes straight back to me.

Note the third part. "You were charged once and only once; I have checked" answers the question they were going to ask tomorrow.

## Record it for the next person

Before you close:

- **Knowledge base**, if it will recur
- **Bug tracker**, if it was a defect — the ticket being closed does not fix the product
- **Category**, corrected if the diagnosis changed it
- **Link** to the incident or issue, so the count is right

<Callout kind="key">
**Closing a ticket is not the same as fixing a problem.**

The customer's instance is resolved. If the cause is still in the product, the next customer meets it next week — and if nobody filed it, support will solve it again from scratch, repeatedly, forever.

A quiet measure of a good support person: the number of problems that stopped arriving because of something they wrote up.
</Callout>

## Follow-up

For anything serious, a short message a few days later:

> Following up on the duplicate charge from last week — can you confirm the refund reached your account? I wanted to check rather than assume.

Thirty seconds. It catches the refunds that did not arrive, and it is the kind of thing customers remember about a company.

## When a customer goes quiet

You asked for a reference and heard nothing.

- Follow up **once**, after a couple of days
- Then say you are closing it, and that replying reopens it
- Then close

> I have not heard back, so I will close this for now. If it happens again, reply here with the reference and this ticket reopens with all the history — nothing is lost.

Not "closing due to no response", which reads as a telling-off. Leave the door open, and say that it is open.
