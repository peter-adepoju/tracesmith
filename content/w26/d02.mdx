Documentation explains what something is. A runbook tells someone what to do at three in the morning when it is broken and they did not build it.

Different document, different reader, different standard.

## The shape

One entry per failure mode:

```
FAILURE   Reconciliation fails: 503 from the provider

LOOKS     Alert in #ops naming "Fetch payouts", HTTP 503 after
LIKE      3 attempts. No summary posted in #finance by 08:10.

MEANS     Today's reconciliation has not run. Finance expects the
          summary by 09:00. Nothing has been written, so a re-run
          is completely safe.

CHECK     1 Provider status page
          2 The execution log — exact error and timings
          3 Whether other workflows on that provider also failed

DO        Provider down: wait. Tell Finance it will be late, with
          a time. Re-run manually once it is back; the workflow is
          idempotent so a re-run creates nothing twice.
          Provider up: the credential may have expired. Check the
          expiry table, rotate if needed, then re-run.

DO NOT    Do not reconcile by hand in a spreadsheet to save time.
          It will not match the automated run and the difference
          will cost more than the delay.
          Do not disable the alert to stop the noise.

RE-RUN    Open the workflow, Execute. Safe at any time. It reads
          only and writes one summary row keyed on the date, so a
          second run for the same date updates rather than
          duplicates.

ESCALATE  Still failing after 2 hours, or it is month end:
          payments team, #inc-payments, tag the duty lead.

OWNER     Peter
```

## The sections that earn their place

**LOOKS LIKE** — so the reader can confirm they are in the right entry. Without it, somebody follows the recovery steps for a different failure.

**MEANS** — the business impact, so they can decide urgency without understanding the system. "Finance expects this by 09:00" is actionable by anyone.

**DO NOT** — the section almost nobody writes, and the one that matters most under pressure. The instinctive shortcut at 3am is usually the thing that makes it worse.

**RE-RUN** — covered below, because it is the question every runbook gets asked and most do not answer.

<Callout kind="key">
**Write DO NOT before DO.**

Under pressure people act fast and reach for the obvious move. Naming the wrong move explicitly — and saying why — is worth more than another paragraph of correct procedure, because the correct procedure was probably going to be followed anyway.

"Do not reconcile by hand" prevents a specific, likely, expensive mistake. That is a better use of two lines than anything else on the page.
</Callout>

## Always say how to re-run safely

Every runbook entry must answer it, because every incident ends with someone asking.

Be specific about **why** it is safe:

> **Safe.** Reads only, and writes one summary row keyed on the date — a second run for the same date updates it rather than creating a duplicate.

> **Safe after 09:00 only.** Before then the provider's batch has not landed and it will reconcile against incomplete data, producing a false discrepancy.

> **Not safe.** This sends emails and has no idempotency key. Re-running will send them again. To recover, identify which merchants were already notified from the run log and re-run only the remainder.

That third one is uncomfortable to write and it is exactly what the person needs. A runbook that says "re-run it" about a workflow that sends emails twice has caused the second incident.

## Three failure modes, minimum

For any workflow, cover at least:

**1. The dependency is down.** External API unavailable or erroring.

**2. The credential failed.** Expired, rotated, revoked, wrong environment.

**3. It did not run at all.** Paused, deleted, trigger broken, platform down. Week 22 day 5 — and this is the entry people forget, because it is the failure that produces no alert from the workflow itself.

Add any mode you have actually seen. A failure that has happened once will happen again.

## Prove it by following it

A runbook that has never been followed is a guess about what would help.

Test it the way week 22 day 6 tested the alerting: cause the failure, hand the runbook to someone else, and watch them follow it without helping.

You will find:

- A step that assumes access they do not have
- A link that is wrong
- A name that changed
- A step that is obvious to you and meaningless to them
- A missing step you do without thinking

Fix all of it. Then **date the page and record who tested it** — an untested runbook and a tested one look identical, and only one of them works.

## Keep it where the alert is

Put the link in the alert itself:

> `Finance: daily reconciliation` failed at `Fetch payouts` — HTTP 503 after 3 attempts.
> **Runbook:** [link]
> Execution 88412: [link]

The person reading at 03:00 should not have to find anything. One click from the alert to the instructions is the difference between a five-minute recovery and a twenty-minute search followed by waking someone up.
