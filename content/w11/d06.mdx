Everything from this week, applied three times. Find three defects, write three reports, grade each one, and fix what the grader catches.

## How today works

Open the lab in one tab and keep this page in another. For each round:

1. Find a defect in Meridian.
2. Reduce it to the shortest steps that still trigger it.
3. Capture the evidence, redacted.
4. Write the report in the grader below.
5. Fix every finding, then re-check until it passes.

Three rounds. Do not shortcut round three because the first two went well — the third is where the habit forms.

## Round 1 — a visible defect

Pick something you can see on screen: a wrong total, a missing message, a value that does not update.

Target: **every structure check green**. Title, environment, steps, expected, actual, evidence, severity and priority all present.

## Round 2 — a defect you had to dig for

Now one that does not show on screen. Use the Network panel: a wrong status, a response field that does not match the display, a request fired twice.

Two things to get right this time:

- Your **evidence must narrow ownership** — the response, not just the screen.
- Your **severity must not follow visibility**. An invisible data defect usually outranks a visible cosmetic one.

<Callout kind="job">
A reviewer's shortcut for round 2: can they tell from your report alone whether this is a front-end or a back-end ticket? If not, the evidence is not doing its job yet.
</Callout>

## Round 3 — an intermittent one

Hardest, and the one that most separates a junior report from a competent one.

Find something that does not happen every time — a double-submit, a race under throttling, a stale value after a back navigation. Then:

- **Count it.** "4 of 25 attempts", never "sometimes".
- Say what you **ruled out**: fresh session, throttling, different product, second browser.
- If throttling makes it reliable, say so — that is a diagnosis, not just a repro.

An honest "not reliably reproduced, here is the frequency and here is what I eliminated" is a good report. A vague "it sometimes breaks" is not.

## Grading yourself afterwards

The grader checks what a machine can check. These it cannot, so ask them yourself:

- Could someone who has never seen Meridian follow my steps?
- Does my expected result say where the expectation comes from — a spec, a convention, a comparison?
- Would I be comfortable if the engineer read this in front of me?
- Is there anything in my evidence I would not want in a public ticket?

That last one is the only question with a hard answer. If yes, remove it before the report leaves your hands.
