A bug does not simply get fixed. It travels through stages, and knowing them tells you what you should be doing at each point — including the one stage that is yours alone.

## The stages

**New** — you found it and wrote it up.

**Triaged** — confirmed by someone, given a severity and a priority, and assigned. Some teams call this Open or Confirmed.

**Assigned** — an engineer owns it.

**In progress** — they are working on it.

**Fixed** — they believe it is done. **This is not the end**, and treating it as the end is the single most common process mistake.

**Retest** — you check. Yours.

**Closed** — you verified the fix.

**Reopened** — you verified and it is still broken, so back it goes.

Two outcomes that are not failures:

**Rejected / Not a bug** — working as designed, or a misunderstanding. If this happens often to your reports, the problem is usually unclear acceptance criteria rather than you.

**Deferred / Won't fix** — real, acknowledged, not worth fixing now. A legitimate business decision, and worth recording so it is not rediscovered every quarter.

<Callout kind="key">
**Only QA closes a bug.** The person who fixed it cannot grade their own homework, and not because they are untrustworthy — because they test the thing they just changed, in the state they just left it in.

You test it fresh: on a clean environment, following your own steps, as a user would. That is a different test, and it catches fixes that work only on the developer's machine.
</Callout>

## Retesting properly

When something lands in Retest, do three things, in order:

1. **Run your original steps exactly.** Not approximately. The steps that found it are the steps that prove it gone.
2. **Check around it.** A fix is a change, and a change can break something adjacent. If the discount calculation was fixed, check the total, the tax line and the receipt.
3. **Try to break it again, differently.** The fix may handle your exact case and miss the general one. If applying a code twice is now blocked, try three times, try two different codes, try applying and removing and applying.

That third step is what separates retesting from re-running.

<Callout kind="watch">
**Never close a bug you have not personally retested.**

Not because a developer said so, not because the ticket moved, not because the deploy went out. A closed bug is a claim that you checked, and that claim gets relied on.

And always retest on a **clean state**. A fix that works because your browser still holds yesterday's session is not a fix.
</Callout>

## Reopening without friction

Reopening can feel confrontational. It is not, and the wording is what keeps it that way.

> Retested on staging with a fresh session, following the original steps. The second application of SAVE10 is now refused, so that path is fixed. Applying two *different* codes still stacks: SAVE10 then WELCOME5 gives 15%. Reopening for that case — happy to split it into a new ticket if you would rather.

That does four things: says exactly what you tested, credits what was fixed, states precisely what is still wrong, and offers a way forward. Nobody can take offence at it, and nobody has to ask you a follow-up question.

## On the board

Most teams track this on a Jira or Linear board with columns roughly matching the stages. Two habits worth having:

- **Move your own items.** A board nobody updates is worse than no board, because it is confidently wrong.
- **Read the board before standing up.** Knowing what is in Retest means you arrive with something to say.
