A bug you cannot reproduce is a bug that will not be fixed. Not because anyone is lazy — because a developer cannot fix what they cannot see, and cannot know when they have.

## Reproduction steps are the product

Of everything in a report, the steps matter most. They are what turns your discovery into something another person can hold.

The bar: **someone who has never seen this product follows your steps and sees the same thing.**

## Reducing

You found it after twenty minutes of clicking. Nobody will follow twenty minutes. Reduce it.

Work backwards and remove things:

1. Write down everything you did, roughly.
2. Remove a step. Does it still happen?
3. Keep removing until taking anything else away makes it stop.
4. What remains is the minimal reproduction.

This is worth the effort twice over. A four-step repro gets fixed faster than a twenty-step one — **and reducing it usually teaches you what the bug actually is.** When you discover the search filter was irrelevant and the quantity was everything, you have gone from "something is wrong in checkout" to "quantity above 99 breaks the total".

<Callout kind="job">
Reducing is also how you stop reporting two bugs as one. If removing a step changes the symptom rather than removing it, you probably have two defects interacting, and they need separate tickets.
</Callout>

## When it is intermittent

"Sometimes" is the hardest word in a bug report, and the wrong response is to give up.

**Count it.** "Three times in twenty attempts" is a fact. "Sometimes" is a feeling, and the difference is the difference between a report that gets investigated and one that gets closed.

Then look for the hidden variable. Intermittent nearly always means something is varying that you have not noticed:

- **Timing.** Fast clicks versus slow. Does it fail only when you are quick?
- **Order.** Did you do something else first?
- **State.** Fresh session versus one that has been open an hour.
- **Data.** A specific product, a specific customer, an empty cart.
- **Environment.** One server in a pool misbehaving — you met this in week 7 with clock skew.
- **Concurrency.** Two tabs, or two people at once.

<Callout kind="key">
**Throttling turns many intermittent bugs into reliable ones.** Set the Network panel to Slow 4G and the window in which a race condition lives widens from milliseconds to seconds.

If a bug appears under throttling and vanishes without it, you have not only reproduced it — you have diagnosed it. That is a timing bug, and the report should say so.
</Callout>

## When you genuinely cannot

It happens. Report it anyway, honestly, and make it useful:

> **Intermittent, not yet reliably reproduced.** Seen 3 times in roughly 40 checkout attempts on staging, 6–7 Nov. Each time: the cart total was correct on the cart page and NGN 0.00 on the confirmation. Console clean. `POST /api/meridian/orders` returned 201 with the correct `totalMinor`, so the data was right and the confirmation rendered it wrong. Not reproducible on demand; tried throttling, double-submits and a fresh session without success.

That is a genuinely useful report. It gives frequency, a precise symptom, evidence that narrows it to the front end, and a list of what has already been ruled out — so nobody repeats your work.

## Recording the conditions

Capture these every time, because any of them can turn out to be the variable:

- Environment and URL
- Account and role
- Browser and version, desktop or mobile
- Date and time, with timezone — needed to find it in a log
- What you did immediately before
- Whether the session was fresh
