<Callout kind="key">
The skill being built today is not "sending requests". It is **saying what happened in one defensible sentence**. That sentence is the atom of a bug report, a support reply and an escalation. Everything later in this course is built out of it.
</Callout>

## How to write a finding

A finding has three parts and fits on one line:

> **What you did** → **what came back** → **what that means**.

For example:

> `GET /v1/customers` with no Authorization header returned **401 invalid key**, so the request never got as far as permissions — it was rejected on identity.

That is a finding. Note what it does not do: it does not guess at causes, it does not blame anyone, and every claim in it is something the response actually showed.

Compare with a non-finding:

> Customers endpoint is broken.

Nobody can act on that.

## The ten

Work through these in order in the inspector. For each, write your one-line finding **before** you read the explanation you can reveal underneath.

### 1. The baseline

`GET /v1/ping`, no key. What status, and why does this one need no credential?

### 2. Identity missing

`GET /v1/customers`, no key. Which family is the code in, and whose problem is it?

### 3. Identity present, permission absent

`GET /v1/customers` with the **public** key. The code changed from the last one. Explain the change in terms of identity versus permission.

### 4. Right idea, wrong verb

`GET /v1/payments/initiate` with the public key. Why is this code slightly encouraging compared to a 404?

### 5. Wrong address

`GET /v1/customer` with the secret key — note the missing `s`. What is the one-character difference worth, in status terms?

### 6. A body that never parsed

Use the **Broken JSON** scenario. Look carefully at the body: there is a trailing comma. What code, and what did the server *not* get as far as doing?

### 7. A body that parsed and failed

Use **Missing a required field**. Compare this code with the previous one. Which fields does the response name?

### 8. Success with a payload

Use **Start a payment**. Read the Payload tab and the Response tab, and say what the server gave you that you did not send.

### 9. Success that is not good news

`GET /v1/payments/verify/ORDER-1002` with the secret key. The status is 200. Is the customer's money taken? Where exactly does the answer live?

### 10. Not your problem

`GET /v1/boom`. What code, and write the escalation sentence you would send, including what you would quote from the response.

<Callout kind="watch">
Number 9 is the one that catches people. **A 200 means the request succeeded, not that the outcome was good.** The request worked perfectly; the payment it describes is pending. Confusing "the call worked" with "the thing happened" is behind a large share of real support mistakes, and you will meet it again in week 19.
</Callout>

## The pattern underneath

Look back at your ten findings. Every one of them answered the same two questions:

1. **Did the request reach the server, and what did it say back?**
2. **Is this my side or their side?**

That is the diagnostic loop for all three jobs in this course. A tester runs it to write a bug report. A support engineer runs it to route a ticket. An automation engineer builds it into the error handling of a workflow.

You will get faster. You will not need a new method.
