Every request starts with a verb. It tells the server what kind of thing you are trying to do, before it even looks at the path.

There are five you will meet in practice. The whole set takes about ten minutes to learn, and getting one wrong is a genuine defect, not a matter of taste.

## The five

**GET — read something.**
`GET /v1/customers` means "give me the customers". A GET asks and nothing more. It has no body.

**POST — create something, or make something happen.**
`POST /v1/payments/initiate` means "start a payment". A POST carries a body with the details. It is the method behind most forms and most actions.

**PUT — replace something entirely.**
`PUT /v1/customers/cus_01` means "here is the complete new version of this customer; overwrite what you have". Anything you leave out of the body is gone.

**PATCH — change part of something.**
`PATCH /v1/customers/cus_01` with a body of `{"email": "new@example.com"}` means "change only the email, leave everything else". The careful cousin of PUT.

**DELETE — remove something.**
`DELETE /v1/customers/cus_01`. Exactly what it says.

## The rule that matters: safe and idempotent

Two words that sound academic and are not. They are the reason a customer gets charged twice.

**Safe** means the request changes nothing. GET is safe. You can send it a thousand times and the system is exactly as it was.

**Idempotent** means doing it twice has the same effect as doing it once. GET, PUT and DELETE are all idempotent. Deleting a customer twice leaves you with the same result as deleting it once: no customer.

**POST is neither.** POST creates. Two POSTs create two things. Two payment POSTs are two payments.

<Callout kind="key">
This is why a retried payment can charge twice, and why "run it twice and check nothing duplicates" is a test you will write over and over. In week 13 you will test for it deliberately; in week 22 you will design automations that survive it.
</Callout>

## Why a wrong method is a defect

It is tempting to think the method is a label and the path does the real work. It is not.

A GET that changes data is a real bug, because the whole system assumes GET is safe:

- Browsers pre-fetch links to make pages feel fast. A pre-fetched "delete" link deletes.
- Caches store GET responses and replay them. A cached action is an action that happens twice, or not at all.
- Search engine crawlers follow every link they find. A crawler can walk through an admin panel and empty it.

These are not hypothetical. "A crawler deleted our content because delete was a GET link" is a classic incident.

<Callout kind="watch">
If you ever see a URL like `/admin/delete?id=42` that works by being visited, you have found a serious bug. Report it. The method is wrong and the consequences are not small.
</Callout>

## What a wrong method looks like in practice

Send the right path with the wrong verb and a well-built server answers **405 Method Not Allowed**. Tomorrow you will meet that code properly, but you can produce one right now.

## Choosing the right one

A quick way to decide:

| You want to | Method |
| --- | --- |
| Fetch a list or a single item | GET |
| Create something new | POST |
| Trigger an action (send, charge, start) | POST |
| Replace a whole record | PUT |
| Change one or two fields | PATCH |
| Remove a record | DELETE |
