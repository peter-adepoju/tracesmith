A ticket is not a note to yourself. It is a record that a colleague will pick up at 2am during an incident, that will be read in six months when the pattern matters, and that may be quoted back to you by a regulator.

The standard is the same one as week 11, pointed at a colleague: **someone who has never seen this can pick it up and continue without asking you anything.**

## The fields

| Field | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Subject** | The problem, specifically. Not "Issue" |
| **Customer** | Account id, and the tier or plan |
| **Channel** | Where it came from |
| **Category** | What kind of problem this is |
| **Priority** | P1 to P4, with a reason if it is not obvious |
| **Description** | What they reported, in their words |
| **Diagnosis** | What you found, and how |
| **Actions taken** | Every step, with timestamps |
| **Current status** | What is happening right now |
| **Next action and owner** | Who does what next |
| **Closure criteria** | What "done" looks like |

The last two are the ones that get left out, and they are the ones that decide whether the ticket moves when you are not there.

## The subject line

Read in a list of two hundred, exactly like a bug title:

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| Payment issue | Card payments declining for merchant ACC-4421 since 07:50 |
| Customer complaint | Settlement of NGN 4.1m due 6 Nov not received |
| Not working | Webhook delivery stopped at 02:00 for partner `lumen-pay` |
| Urgent!! | Duplicate charge on TRX-8841, refund not yet issued |

Pattern: **what, who, when.** Never put urgency in the subject — that is what the priority field is for, and a subject line shouting at you is noise the twentieth time you scroll past it.

## Write the diagnosis as you go

Not at the end. Each step, as you take it:

```
10:02  Customer reports card declines from ~07:50. ACC-4421.
10:05  Checked transactions for ACC-4421: 14 attempts since 07:48,
       all declined, all provider code 05 (do not honour).
10:09  Checked three other merchants on the same provider route:
       same pattern from 07:48. Not customer-specific.
10:11  Raised INC-204. Linked T-01 and T-03.
10:14  Replied to both merchants with acknowledgement and a
       15:00 update commitment.
```

Six lines, and anyone can now take over. Note the shape: each line is a **fact with a time**, not a feeling. "Checked three other merchants" is reproducible; "seems widespread" is not.

## Categories

Boring, and the thing that makes your queue data worth having:

- Payment failure
- Settlement or payout
- Refund or dispute
- Account or access
- Integration or API
- Reporting or data
- How-to or documentation
- Bug (confirmed)
- Feature request

Two habits:

**Categorise accurately even when it is slower.** Six months of accurate categories tells the company that a third of tickets are one integration problem, and that is a roadmap item. Six months of "Other" tells it nothing.

**Re-categorise when the diagnosis changes.** A "payment failure" that turns out to be a webhook problem is a webhook ticket now.

<Callout kind="job">
If a category is missing — and it usually is — raise that. "We have no category for duplicate charges and I have seen nine this month" is a small, concrete, visible contribution that makes you useful beyond your own queue.
</Callout>

## Ownership

**One owner at a time, always a human.** A ticket owned by "the team" is owned by nobody, which is the mechanism by which things sit for a week.

A handoff has three parts, and all three are needed:

1. **A summary in the ticket** — what is done, what is next, what you are waiting for.
2. **An explicit change of owner** in the system.
3. **A message to that person**, so they know it exists.

Doing only the third is how a ticket lives in a chat thread and nowhere else.

## Writing the handoff

> Handing to Chidi for the evening. Customer ACC-4421, card declines since 07:50, part of INC-204 with T-03. Provider confirmed a routing issue at 11:20 and estimates a fix by 16:00. I have told both merchants we will update at 15:00 — **that update is the next action and it is due whether or not the provider has come back.** If the fix lands, confirm with the merchants that payments are going through before closing; do not close on the provider's word alone.

That carries the state, the commitment with its deadline, and the closure criteria — including the instruction not to close on someone else's say-so, which is the week 11 retest rule in support clothing.

## Closure criteria, decided at the start

Write what "done" means before you begin:

- The customer confirms it works
- The refund appears in their account, verified in the system
- The webhook has delivered successfully and the partner confirms receipt

Without this, tickets close on *"I think that is sorted"* and reopen three days later — and a reopened ticket costs far more than the five minutes of verification would have.
