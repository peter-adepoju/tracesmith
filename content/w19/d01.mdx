Most technical support jobs in Nigeria sit near payments, and the questions customers ask are nearly all variations on four or five things. This week makes you fluent in them.

## The four parties

**The customer** — holds a card issued by their bank.

**The merchant** — the business taking the payment. Your customer, usually.

**The acquirer** — the merchant's bank, which receives the money.

**The issuer** — the customer's bank, which holds the money and decides whether to release it.

Plus the **card network** (Visa, Mastercard, Verve) routing between the two banks, and the **gateway** or payment service provider sitting in front of all of it.

## What a gateway does, and does not

**Does:** collects the card details securely so the merchant never touches them, routes the request to the right acquirer, returns a result, notifies the merchant by webhook, and manages settlement, refunds and reporting.

**Does not:** decide whether the payment succeeds.

<Callout kind="key">
**The gateway never approves or declines a payment. The issuing bank does.**

This single fact answers a surprising share of angry tickets. When a customer says "your system declined my card", it did not — their own bank did, and the gateway passed the message along.

The gateway usually cannot even see *why*. It receives a reason code, and the issuer is under no obligation to be specific.
</Callout>

## Two separations that explain almost everything

**Authorisation is not settlement.** The money is held at step 4 and moves at step 8, usually the next working day. Tomorrow's lesson.

**The browser return is not the notification.** The customer's redirect back to the shop is a convenience. The webhook is the record. Day 3.

Nearly every confusing payment ticket is one of those two being conflated by somebody.

## Vocabulary you will use daily

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Authorisation** | The issuer agrees and holds the funds. No money has moved |
| **Capture** | The merchant claims the held funds. Often automatic |
| **Settlement** | Money actually reaches the merchant's bank. Batched |
| **Reference** | The id for one payment. Always get this |
| **Reason code** | The issuer's terse explanation for a decline |
| **Chargeback** | The customer disputes it with their bank and the money is pulled back |
| **Minor units** | Money as integers. NGN 48,500.00 is `4850000` |

## Why money is never a float

You met this in week 13 and it matters more here than anywhere.

`0.1 + 0.2` is `0.30000000000000004` in essentially every language. Across a day of transactions those fractions accumulate, and a settlement that is off by a few kobo is a reconciliation nobody can close.

So amounts are integers of the smallest unit: kobo for naira, cents for dollars. `4850000` is NGN 48,500.00.

**When a response shows an amount with a decimal point, that is worth a second look.** It is either a display value or a defect, and the difference matters.
