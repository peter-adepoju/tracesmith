Customers use all three words for the same thing. They are different mechanisms with different costs, different timelines and different people in charge — and the difference matters a great deal to the merchant.

## Refund

The merchant chooses to give the money back. Voluntary, controlled, cheap.

- Initiated by the merchant
- Full or partial
- Typically three to five working days to reach the customer's account
- Costs the processing fee, which is usually not returned
- No penalty beyond that

A refund is the good outcome. Everything below is worse for everyone.

## Chargeback

The customer goes to **their own bank** and disputes the charge. The bank pulls the money back from the merchant, often before anyone has asked the merchant what happened.

- Initiated by the customer, through their bank
- The merchant finds out after the money is gone
- Carries a **fee** — frequently in the thousands of naira — whether or not the merchant eventually wins
- Counts against the merchant's chargeback ratio, and too high a ratio puts their ability to accept cards at risk
- Timeline runs for weeks

## Dispute

The process of contesting a chargeback. The merchant submits evidence — delivery proof, logs, correspondence, terms accepted — and the issuer decides.

So: **a refund is a decision, a chargeback is a demand, and a dispute is the argument about a chargeback.**

<Callout kind="key">
**A refund now is almost always cheaper than a chargeback later.**

A merchant arguing over a NGN 10,000 refund can lose the money anyway through a chargeback, plus a fee of similar size, plus a mark on a ratio that eventually threatens their card acceptance entirely.

When a merchant hesitates over a small refund, this is the argument — and it is a commercial one, not a moral one.
</Callout>

## The chargeback timeline

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Day 0** | Customer disputes with their bank |
| **Days 1–3** | Issuer raises it; funds are debited from the merchant |
| **Day 3** | Merchant is notified, after the money has gone |
| **Days 3–X** | Merchant submits evidence, usually within 7 to 14 days |
| **Weeks 2–8** | Issuer decides |
| **If won** | Funds returned. The fee usually is not |
| **If lost** | Funds stay with the customer |
| **Rarely** | A second round, or arbitration |

Two things to tell merchants early: **the deadline is short and it is fatal.** Miss the evidence window and they lose automatically, however good their case was.

## What makes good chargeback evidence

- Proof of delivery, signed where possible
- The customer's acceptance of terms, with a timestamp
- Correspondence showing the issue was raised and handled
- IP address and device matching previous legitimate orders
- The authorisation record with the AVS and CVV results
- For digital goods: access logs showing the product was used

Support's job is usually to **gather** this rather than to argue it, and the gathering is time-bound.

## Common reasons, and what they really mean

| Reason given | Often actually |
| --- | --- |
| Fraudulent transaction | A family member used the card, or they did not recognise the name on the statement |
| Product not received | A delivery problem, or a slow one |
| Product not as described | A real dispute about quality |
| Duplicate charge | Frequently genuine, and frequently our fault |
| Subscription not cancelled | A cancellation flow nobody can find |

The first row is worth knowing. A large share of "fraud" chargebacks are **friendly fraud** — the customer genuinely does not recognise `SPLTPAY*LAGOS` on a statement. A clear, recognisable descriptor prevents more chargebacks than any dispute process wins.

## Handling a dispute between two parties

Sometimes the merchant and the customer disagree and you are in the middle.

- **Stay factual.** You hold the records: what was charged, when, what was delivered, what was said.
- **Do not take a side**, and do not pretend not to have the evidence.
- **Share what each party is entitled to see**, and no more. The customer does not get the merchant's internal notes.
- **Know your limits.** A genuine commercial dispute between two businesses is not support's to settle.

> I can confirm what our records show: a payment of NGN 48,500 authorised on 6 November at 14:02, reference `PAY-8841`, and a refund of NGN 20,000 released on 9 November, reference `REF-2291`. The remaining NGN 28,500 has not been refunded.
>
> Whether the rest is owed is a matter between you and the merchant, and I cannot decide it. What I can do is make sure both of you have the same facts, which is what I have sent to them as well.
