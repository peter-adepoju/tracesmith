"I paid but my order says unpaid" is the commonest hard ticket in payments, and it almost always comes down to three mechanisms that people treat as interchangeable.

They are not interchangeable, and only one of them is the truth.

## The three

**The browser return.** After paying, the customer is redirected back to the shop with something in the URL. Fast, visible, and **worthless as evidence** — it comes from the customer's browser, so it can be lost, duplicated, bookmarked, or edited by hand.

**The webhook.** The gateway makes a server-to-server call to the merchant with the result. Reliable, signed, retried, and invisible to the customer. **This is the notification.**

**Verification.** The merchant asks the gateway directly: *what is the status of reference `PAY-8841`?* Always available, always current. **This is the truth.**

<Callout kind="key">
**Verification is the source of truth. The webhook is the notification. The browser return is a convenience.**

A merchant who creates orders from the browser return has built a shop where closing the tab loses the order — and where anyone who reads the URL can create a paid order without paying.

A merchant who creates orders from the webhook alone is nearly right, and should still verify before shipping anything expensive.
</Callout>

## Why a browser return proves nothing

It arrives from the customer's machine. Which means it can:

- **Not arrive at all** — tab closed, signal lost, battery died
- **Arrive twice** — refresh, back button
- **Be replayed** — a bookmarked success URL, used again tomorrow
- **Be forged** — `?status=success&ref=PAY-9999` typed by hand

The correct pattern is: the return tells the page what to *display*; the webhook tells the server what *happened*; verification confirms it before anything irreversible.

## Diagnosing "paid but not marked paid"

In order:

1. **Verify at the gateway.** Look the reference up. Did it succeed? This is the only question that settles it.
2. **Check webhook delivery.** Was it attempted? What did the merchant's endpoint return?
3. **Check the merchant's end.** Did they receive it and fail to process it?
4. **Check what they built on.** If their order creation depends on the redirect, that is the bug.

Four outcomes:

| Finding | Meaning | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not successful at the gateway | It genuinely failed | Explain; customer is not charged |
| Successful, webhook never sent | Our side | Escalate to the gateway team |
| Successful, webhook sent, merchant returned 5xx | Their endpoint | Tell them, re-send |
| Successful, webhook delivered 200, no order | Their processing | Their bug; give them the payload |

That table is most of payment support.

## Signature verification

Webhooks carry a signature computed from the payload and a shared secret. The merchant must verify it.

A merchant who does not is accepting any POST to that URL as a real payment notification — so anyone who knows the URL can create paid orders for free. When a merchant asks why their webhook handler should bother, that is the answer.

<Callout kind="watch">
Two things merchants get wrong constantly:

**Returning 200 before processing.** The gateway treats 200 as *received and handled* and stops retrying. If they then fail to process it, the event is gone and nothing will send it again. Acknowledge after processing, or queue it first.

**Not handling duplicates.** Retries mean the same event can arrive more than once, and so can a legitimate re-send. Handlers must be idempotent — week 13's lesson, now with money attached.
</Callout>

## The reply

> I have checked with the gateway and your payment of NGN 48,500 succeeded at 14:02 — reference `PAY-8841`. You have been charged once and you will not be charged again.
>
> What failed is the message that tells the shop about it: we attempted delivery three times and their system returned an error each time, so it never learned about your payment.
>
> I have let the merchant know and your order is being created now. I will confirm within the hour.

Three things: they are charged, they will not be charged again, and someone is fixing it. No mention of webhooks, signatures or retries — none of that is theirs to carry.
