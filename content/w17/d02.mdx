Seeing it yourself turns a report into a fact. The difficulty is that a customer's problem lives in their conditions, not yours.

## Rebuild the conditions

A failure is a function of more than the steps:

- **Account** — their role, their plan, their permissions, their configuration
- **Data** — their specific records. An account with 40,000 transactions behaves differently from a fresh one
- **Device and browser** — including the engine, from week 12
- **Network** — mobile data in a poor-signal area is not your office wifi
- **Timing** — month end, a settlement window, a promotion
- **History** — what they did before the thing that failed

Most "works for me" is one of these differing and nobody noticing which.

<Callout kind="watch">
**Never reproduce on a live customer account.**

No test payments, no changing a setting "just to see", no placing an order. It is their money, their data, and an action you take on their account is indistinguishable in the record from an action they took.

Read their record freely. Act on a test account.

If the only way to verify is an action with consequences, that is an escalation, not an experiment — and if a customer *asks* you to try something on their account, get it in writing in the ticket.
</Callout>

## The ladder

In order of preference:

1. **Test account on staging.** Free, safe, no consequences. Start here.
2. **Test account on production.** Real conditions, real integrations, and a real audit trail — so behave accordingly. Many payment problems only exist here.
3. **Reading the customer's record.** Not reproduction, but often better: their transactions, their logs, their timeline.
4. **A screen-share, with their permission.** For a problem that will not reproduce any other way.
5. **Asking them to try once more while you watch the logs.** Only with their agreement, and only when nothing else works.

Steps 3 and 4 are underrated. For most payment tickets, reading the record answers the question faster than any reproduction would.

## "Works for me"

This is a result, not a dead end. It tells you the failure depends on something that differs between you and them — so the job is now to find the difference.

Work through it in order:

- Their **account** versus your test account — permissions, plan, settings
- Their **data** — volume, an unusual character in a name, an old record in a state yours has never been in
- Their **browser** — Safari, iOS, an old version, an extension
- Their **network** — a corporate proxy, mobile data, a blocked domain
- **Timing** — did it work before a specific date? What changed then?
- Their **sequence** — did they do something before this that you did not?

<Callout kind="key">
**"Works for me" is never an answer to the customer.**

To them it means *I do not believe you*, and they are not entirely wrong — it is a statement about you rather than about their problem.

Say instead: *"I have not been able to reproduce it on my test account yet, which usually means something is specific to your setup. Can I check a couple of things with you?"* Same fact, and it keeps you both pointed at the problem.
</Callout>

## Reproducing from a log

Sometimes you cannot reproduce anything and the record still tells you everything: the request arrived at 14:02, the provider answered `insufficient_funds` at 14:03, our record says `successful`. That is a complete diagnosis without a single reproduction.

Reproduction is a means. The end is knowing what happened.
