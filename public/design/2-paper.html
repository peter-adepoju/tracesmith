Writing a report that needs no follow-up

This is the most valuable thing a tester produces. Not the bug — the report.

The standard is one sentence long: an engineer should be able to act on it without asking you anything. Everything below is in service of that one test, and a report that fails it has failed whatever else it does well.

The shape

Title       Applying the same discount code twice stacks
            the reduction (cart)

Environment Meridian sandbox, stage 3 · Chrome 131 · 7 Nov

Steps       1. Add Harmattan Field Jacket to the cart
            2. Open the cart
            3. Enter SAVE10 and click Apply
            4. Enter SAVE10 again and click Apply

Expected    The second application is refused; the total
            stays at NGN 43,650.00

Actual      The discount becomes 20%. The total drops to
            NGN 38,800.00. Ten applications reach NGN 0.00

Severity    Critical          Priority  Highest

Read it as an engineer: you know what to do, where to go, what you will see, and what it should have been. Nothing is missing, and you did not have to ask.

The title does most of the work

It is read in a list of a hundred, in a notification, in a stand-up. The pattern is what happens, where, and under what condition.

WeakStrong
Discount bugSame code applied twice stacks the reduction

Tone

You are describing a system, never a person. “Applying the same code twice stacks the reduction” gets fixed faster than “you broke the discount logic” — not because engineers are fragile, but because a report that reads as an accusation invites a defence, and a defence is a delay.

Never put urgency in the subject That is what the priority field is for. A title shouting at you is noise the twentieth time you scroll past it.
The sentence people leave out When severity and priority differ, say why. Without it someone reads “Critical” and “Low” together and comes to ask; with it, the trade-off is visible and agreed.
