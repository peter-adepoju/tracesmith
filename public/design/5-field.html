Writing a report that needs no follow-up

  • Write all seven sections well
  • Title a bug so it is findable
  • Judge your own report against the no-questions test

This is the most valuable thing a tester produces. Not the bug — the report.

The standard is one sentence long: an engineer should be able to act on it without asking you anything.

The shape

Title       Applying the same discount code twice stacks
            the reduction (cart)

Environment Meridian sandbox, stage 3 · Chrome 131 · 7 Nov

Steps       1. Add Harmattan Field Jacket to the cart
            2. Enter SAVE10 and click Apply
            3. Enter SAVE10 again and click Apply

Expected    Refused; the total stays at NGN 43,650.00
Actual      Accepted; the total drops to NGN 38,800.00
            Ten applications reach NGN 0.00

Severity    Critical          Priority  Highest

Read it as an engineer: you know what to do, where to go, what you will see, and what it should have been.

The title does most of the work

Title
✕Discount bug
✓Same discount code applied twice stacks the reduction
✕Payment issue
✓Double-clicking Place order creates two orders and charges twice
NOTE — THE SENTENCE PEOPLE LEAVE OUT

When severity and priority differ, say why. One line, and it prevents the argument: “Critical severity, low priority — only two internal users reach it and exporting in two halves works.”

Tone

You are describing a system, never a person. A report that reads as an accusation invites a defence, and a defence is a delay.

Wording
✕You broke the discount logic
✓Applying the same code twice stacks the reduction
✕Obviously nobody tested this
✓Not covered by the existing cases; I have added DISC-003
